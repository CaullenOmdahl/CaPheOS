# Accountable leads (owner, 2026-11-19), built on wave 3 before the sandbox rollout

Owner, verbatim:
- "I just want to ensure that we have the delegation pathway proper, so we have like a proper company tier where like you have the middle management leads, and they're going to be fully responsible for the employees that work underneath them and produce work, and then they're going to review the work that was done and ensure quality, and then pass it to where it needs to go, correct? Like the big thing with the middle management is that it should have accountability."
- "I believe it would still be cheaper for the lead to audit the work rather than having the lead write the work and also audit the work and then have another high class model get the second opinion on the work. In this model, the lead delegates the work and then the local model, like the lower model, checks its own work and then the lead also checks its work. So that should be about around a 75% reduction in overall usage or cost based on how the models are tiered."

## The pathway
1. **Intake through the lead.** Work addressed to a department lands with its lead. The lead assigns it to a member, or does it only when no member fits. Work sent straight to a member is copied to the lead and counts as the lead's.
2. **Workers on the lower tier.** Members run on cheaper or local models (routing classes and tier caps; code is never on MiniMax). The lead sits on the top tier and mainly reads.
3. **The worker checks its own work first.** Before handing work back, it runs a self-check against the item's acceptance check: its tests, the check command, and a short "what I checked" note. Work that fails its own check never reaches the lead.
4. **The lead's audit is binding.** Every finished item goes to the lead. The lead answers ACCEPT, or REVISE with what to fix. The dispatcher holds the onward handoff, the merge, and the item's DONE until the lead accepts.
   - The audit packet is the diff plus the acceptance check plus the self-check note, never the whole history.
   - Routine items can be audited on a mid tier; risky items get the lead's top-tier model.
   - Later, per department and earned through the scorecard: spot-checks for reliable members on low-risk work.
5. **The lead passes accepted work on.** The receiver deals with one accountable person.
6. **Accountability: a department scorecard on the lead.**
   - It measures on time, sent-back rate, cost per accepted item, and "escapes": problems found later in work the lead accepted, which are charged to the lead's review.
   - The scorecard shows on the department card in the console.
   - The lead's standing is the department's results.
   - The people function uses it when it reviews the lead.
7. **Escalation one level at a time.** Member to lead; lead to CEO only for cross-department issues or the four fixed owner matters.
8. **Measure the saving.** model-rank compares cost per accepted item against the same work done by the lead alone. The owner expects about a 75% cut. If the saving doesn't show, the department falls back to advisor mode and the owner is told in plain words.
9. **No second high-tier opinion by default.** That is the cost the owner wants gone.
